# Bookmarks Folder, Spreadsheet, or Channel Organizer? Choosing Honestly

This guide is written with one purpose: to help you pick a channel-management approach **without giving you a sales pitch**. The three options below all exist for good reasons. The right answer depends on how many channels you track, whether you are the only person involved, and what you do with the list afterwards.

## Option 1: Browser bookmarks (folders in your browser)

**What it's good at.** Nearly nothing — but with that said, browser bookmarks are the simplest way to *store links*. Folders in your browser are easy to create and restructure as your needs change, and nothing else needs installing.

**Where it breaks.** Bookmarks are **link-shaped**. YouTube channel pages contain "extra cruft" in the URLs (parameters, sorting, tracking tags) — these go in the bookmark silently and make the list disappear from view as a coherent list. There are no channel identities, just titles without structure: your "Watch" folder becomes an alphabet-soup of truncated URLs; there's no data about what each channel subscribers irrelevant to the real question of whether a channel is still active. And there is no **meaningful sharing**: a bookmarks export is a file of HTML links your teammates will not thank you for.

**Choose it when:** you have fewer than a dozen channels, you are alone, and you never plan to share.

## Option 2: Spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel, Notion)

**What it's good at.** Spreadsheets win on **flexibility**: free-form columns, sorting, filters, notes ("checked on May 3rd"), color codes, and sheet-level filtering with which anyone used to spreadsheets is instantly productive. As a *record-keeping tool for audits*, spreadsheets are serious capabilities.

**Where it breaks.** The spreadsheet is a **database of the past**. It does not know today's state: subscriber counts, upload activity, and channel identity (avatar, name changes) exist only as stale text someone typed. It has, of course, no relationships with YouTube itself — for the curation of *sources*, this means constant manual syncing. And sharing, though possible, invites the classic versioning problem: three people have three files, and none is the current one. The sheet's second-worst trait is *effort*: the manual work of maintaining it grows linearly with the number of channels.

**Choose it when:** you need audit columns strongly personalized (dates, notes, flags) and you like that level of weekly maintenance; you're prepared to be, effectively, the spreadsheet's operator.

## Option 3: Dedicated channel organizers (what Groupify is)

**What it's good at.** A channel organizer treats curation as an ongoing state: channels are **first-class objects** (names, avatars, subscriber counts, activity) in a structure (groups, nested categories) that stays current as you use it. Capture is cheap — the browser extension saves a channel with a single click while you are on its YouTube page. Share links show a **live view** of the current state, not an old export file. Team roles match how curation teams actually work (view-only, editor, admin).

**Where it's weak.** Honest assessment: **flexibility of schema is its achilles heel.** A spreadsheet lets you add "verified-by" and "rev counter" columns; an organizer is built around channels and groups, not arbitrary metadata. If your use case is genuinely audit-shaped (compliance, archival), keep the spreadsheet — use an organizer for the *curation-of-sources* part, or don't, honestly — spreadsheets are not the wrong answer universally.

**Another honest limitation: lock-in risk.** A browser tool deserves scrutiny about what happens when you don't want it to be the center of your life. Which leads to the question underneath —

## The question that decides everything: portability

Whatever tool you pick, ask one question before investing weekend hours: **can I get my data out, in a usable form, without re-typing?**

- Bookmarks export: HTML links — poor but possible.
- Spreadsheets: trivially portable (it IS the file); the strongest point in their column.
- Organizers: varies wildly. In Groupify's case, **export of your groups is a supported feature** and our share-and-copy workflow means migration between workspaces is a copy operation, not a migration project.

This question is the real dividing line in serious use. A curated collection is a growing asset; a tool that cannot hand it back treats your effort as its own.

## Summary table

| Approach | Number of channels | Keeping data current | Sharing with a team | Suggested for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bookmarks | under ~15 | passive (links only) | poor | solo, casual |
| Spreadsheet | 15–100 | manual sync; slips quickly | file-sharing + drift mess | audit-shaped needs, analysts |
| Channel organizer | any scale | live (capture via extension) | live links with roles | active curators, teams, communities |

Read this as a decision aid — not as a ranking. If your channel life is compact and audit-shaped with small numbers, do not feel pushed into adopting dedicated tools. The honest answer is: match the tool's *shape* to your *purpose*.
