# The Groupify Browser Extension: Save YouTube Channels Without Leaving YouTube

Most channel-organizing systems fail at the moment of capture. You watch a video, you like the channel, you think "I should save this" — and then the thought dies because saving means opening a new tab, logging into something, finding the right list, and pasting a URL. Two weeks later you rememberThinking that channel existed and cannot find it anywhere.

Groupify's browser extension exists to remove that friction: while you're on YouTube, one click saves the channel you're looking at into whichever group you choose. You never leave the page, and your future self has one place where every interesting channel lives.

## How capturing works in practice

A typical session looks like this:

1. **You are watching a video** — say, a channel review that recommends three channels you want to explore later.
2. **You click the Groupify icon** in your browser's toolbar while viewing any of those channels' pages.
3. **A small panel appears** showing your recent groups. You pick one — e.g. "Sci-Fi Lore" — click save, and the panel confirms: *"Channel added to group."*
4. **You go back to watching.** The capture took about four seconds, and — importantly — did not require you to decide anything about the *video*, just the *channel*.

The channel lands in your dashboard with its name, avatar, and subscriber count, so your group pages look organized at a glance rather than like a pile of URLs.

## Why the number of clicks matters more than it seems

If you are thinking "I could just note down channel names in a text file", you're right — that works for a while. What breaks these cascading todos is not intellect but friction. A capture system gets used only if the cost of saving is close to zero *at the exact moment you want to save*. The extension moves the cost down to a click; a notepad file sits at "open a new app, find the tab, type it, don't forget the exact spelling".

This is also why the extension pairs so well with the **subscriptions sidebar**. Open YouTube's subscription list, hover through it, and for each channel you actually like, save it into a group. Ten minutes of hovering later, you have a list for every channel you care about in your subscriptions sidebar — ready to be unsubscribed from the old ones without any fear of losing track of them.

## A habit that keeps the system clean

The extension makes saving so easy that the opposite risk appears: **over-collection**. If every marginally interesting channel goes into a group, your groups become a second junk drawer. Three rules keep that in check:

1. **Save with intent, not on impulse.** When the panel opens, ask: "Would I want to see this channel's next upload?" If the answer is "maybe", save to a dedicated "To review" group rather than a curated one.
2. **The "To review" group should be regularly emptied.** Once in a while (weekly is fine), go through it. Promote channels into their real homes or delete them. Unreviewed channels not sitting there for months is what makes the system feel lived-in — and keeps your collections relevant.
3. **One channel may belong to one home** at a time. If two groups both "need" a channel, think about which group carries the *role*; nesting with a shared parent works better than duplicating the channel.

## It's open source — and that matters

The Groupify extension is open source: you can look at the code, self-audit what it does and does not do, and raise issues or contribute on GitHub. We think tools that live across your browsing experience (with permission to act on YouTube pages) should be transparent by default. The readme explains exactly what the extension touches and what data travels where — no black boxes.

If you spot a rough edge while browsing a new YouTube layout (YouTube redesigns things often), the fastest path to a fix is a report in our Discord or on GitHub. Real-world breakage reports from active users are the most valuable bug reports any extension project gets.

## Where to get it

Once you're logged in at groupify.dev, the links to install the extension for your browser exist directly from the dashboard — and the source lives on GitHub. Install it, sign in on the panel, and you're finished; your existing groups and structure are instantly available with no re-setup.
