# Exporting and Backing Up Your Channel Collection Data

Curation is an investment. The list of channels that took you three afternoons to build — the nested structure, the vetting decisions, the share setup — is an asset. This guide covers the habits that keep that asset safe and portable, both inside Groupify and outside it.

## What the export covers

Groupify lets you **export your groups** — the channel collections with their organization — as a file you own. An export is a complete, portable form of your curation: if you leave tomorrow, everything you built travels with you.

We deliberately treat "no-lock-in" as a feature, not a promise. Two reasons legitimize this:

1. **It disciplines us.** When your data can walk, the product has to keep earning your use, instead of assuming it.
2. **It enables recombination.** A portable collection is usable by the wider ecosystem — friends importing your list, agencies handing a structure to the client's own workspace, teams re-splitting an old collection.

## A two-minute backup ritual

Curation data changes slowly, but does change: new channels, removals, restructures. Minimal ritual (we recommend doing it quarterly):

1. **Export** your groups to a file.
2. **Store it like any other backup** — wherever such assets are kept (local folder, workspace, corporate storage; do what fits your security practice).
3. **Safeguard the file** with a small note: the date, and (for teams) who exported it.

Two minutes per quarter is low friction for the peace of mind that your structure cannot be lost to an account mishap, a browser cleanup, or a "who deleted the group?" moment.

## Migration pattern: from one workspace into another

When moving — from personal workspace to team workspace, or from one project's space to another — the flow in Groupify is built around the same share mechanism you use for teams:

1. In the new (or receiving) workspace holder's account, open the share link of the group being transferred.
2. **Copy the collection** into their workspace; now they hold their own copy with every structure included.
3. The original workspace holder can then retire the old group, unshare the link, or simply keep both versions running long enough that teammates are sure they've adopted the new one.

For a personal migration, you can follow both routes freely — **share-and-copy beats manual re-entry** for any collection beyond trivial size; and where decisions are needed (which channels survive, whether names change), you make them *after* everything has been moved, not before.

## When exporting is also the input to other tools

A list of channels is a building-block in different content workflows — "give me all managed/related creators in aCSV," team project boards, community vetting documents. A file-level export keeps you free to take the data to those tools rather than reconstructing it manually at each step.

The general principle, stated as a pledge: **the collection is yours; the tool is a convenience around it.** If both are always true, the effort you invest in your curation compounds over the years instead of becoming hostage to a subscription.

## One practical note

If you're evaluating whether curation tooling is worth adopting at all (we wrote about exact benefits in other guides), put export support at the top of your checklist. Tools that make leaving easy are tools that were confident enough to make staying honest.
