# Organizing Your YouTube Subscriptions Into Groups: A Practical Guide

Every YouTube account drifts toward the same destination: a subscriptions page you never open, thirty browser tabs of "watch later", and the feeling that channels you actively want to watch are buried under channels you subscribed to once in 2021 and forgot about.

The instinctive responses are deleting subscriptions in bulk or ignoring the problem. A better response is to restructure. This is a practical guide to doing exactly that — and it works whether you use Groupify, a spreadsheet, or a paper notebook.

Think of the "subscription page" methodology carefully, and you will find it is not designed to be used. It is a mixed bag of content: hobby channels, channels run by friends and coworkers, big corporate channels, and channels that stopped uploading years ago. Every video in this stream lives in the same place and has two things in common: the channels are "subscribed" to and were uploaded "recently". The other questions — "will this be interesting to me", "in what state am I right now" — are yours to remember. When your subscriptions list is organized, the stream is again arranged, but the questions remain.

## Step 1: Distinguish your channels by *role*, not by *appeal*

When reorganizing, the biggest mistake people make is trying to categorize channels by "genre", producing unsatisfying results ("education", "entertainment", "lifestyle", children, etc.). The more useful level of organization is **what role the channel plays in your week**:

- **Instruments for learning** — The channels that influence how you work (deep tutorials, documentation-style re-broadcasts, conference talks).
- **Leisure** — Things you emotionally enjoy (comedy, commentary, reaction videos, gaming).
- **Producers** — People you follow for their next thing (music artists you want to support, indie creators whose launch you want to see).
- **Reference** — how-to channels you visit only when the need arises, not in your daily watching flow.
- **Social** — colleagues, friends, communities you follow to stay in touch with people or fans.

These categories do not match the way YouTube's algorithm sorts by content type. But they exactly match your real-world weekly behavior, making them a much better structural principle — a "chronological" group by month.

## Step 2: Build groups by "when and why I go there"

Once you know the role of any channel, create groups corresponding to those small moments in your life, that is: "choose to watch". Then form groups that correspond to those situations. Some combinations that have worked for users:

- **"Deep work block"** — channels you visit when you have an hour of full attention a week to spare.
- **"Fifteen in between minutes"** — short videos to kill time while waiting, without engaging your mind.
- **"Family — safe"** — you hand this to your partner or kid with confidence. Everything is vetted.
- **"Weekend cooking"** — the getting-late-evening channel, for Saturdays.
- **"Following"** — channels whose uploads you want to know about, even though you do not watch everything.

Note how concrete these names are. "Technology" is not; "Watch while eating lunch" is. Groups named by situations allow you to immediately orient and find a channel in a few seconds.

## Step 3: Be merciful toward old subscriptions

As you move channels into groups, you will inevitably come across old subscriptions you have forgotten. Make a decision about each one: **the group it belongs to** — or unsubscribe from it.

If you cannot classify a channel in about five seconds, remove it from your subscription list. It will reappear in your recommendations should you actually like a new visible video, and a group-based organizational structure only works if each item in it earns a place.

## Step 4: Maintain the rhythm

Dedicate a few minutes once a week to sweep through new finds. The group checklist is short:

1. Is there a channel I came across this week that has yet to be grouped? Put it somewhere.
2. Is there a group that has grown too cluttered? Split it, or make a nested group within it.
3. Is there a channel that no longer genuinely interests me? Then it wanders off to the unsubscribe pile.

This weekly upkeep is a much smaller task than the first full reorganization, and the payoff is that the system stays accurate.
