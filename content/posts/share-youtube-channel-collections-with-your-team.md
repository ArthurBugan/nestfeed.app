# How to Share YouTube Channel Collections With Your Team

Somewhere in your team's workflow, someone maintains a list of channels. A research team keeps its sources. An agency keeps its client-relevant channels. A mod team for a Discord community keeps its vetted creators. The list lives in a Google Sheet with URL columns, or a doc, or a message thread with "I'll add you tomorrow" promises — and it is always out of date.

Sharing curated lists properly is a solved problem, but only if you accept two conditions: the list must have **one source of truth**, and members must be able to **use** the list rather than copy-paste chunks of it into spreadsheets. Here is how that workflow looks in Groupify, and how to adapt it to your team.

## The basic mechanism: share links

Every group in Groupify can produce one or more **share links**. A share link is a URL that opens a read-structured view of the group: the channels, organized as they are organized in the owner's workspace. People who open the link see the collection — the channels, avatars, and subscriber counts — and can interact based on the permissions you assigned to that link or role.

The important design distinction is **viewing vs copying**:

- **View** is for consumers: members who need to *consult* the list (e.g. "which channels are approved sources this week?"). They see current state every time they open the link — no stale copies, no version confusion.
- **Copy** is for adopters: someone who wants the collection imported into their own workspace to maintain their own version — a great experience for communities where members want to fork your curated tech-channels list into their own dashboard and customize it from there.

Because links reflect live state, there is exactly one source of truth. When someone adds a channel, everyone sees it the next time they open the link. That alone removes the "which spreadsheet is current?" problem that plagues team lists.

## Roles: how much control to hand out

When the collection is more than a handout — when teammates are *maintaining* it — permissions matter. Groupify groups support roles:

- **View-only** — can see the collection. Right for audiences and stakeholders.
- **Editor** — can add/remove/rearrange channels. Right for working members of the curation team.
- **Admin** — full control of the group, including its structure and sharing. Right for owners and leads.

A common team setup looks like this: one or two admins own the workspace; a handful of editors maintain categories; everyone else views via link. Editors make changes; admins review the group page periodically; viewers always see the latest.

## An adoption pattern: the "curated list meeting"

For teams that maintain the collection collectively, a small meeting ritual keeps the system healthy without meetings-about-meetings:

1. **Anyone can nominate** a channel — drop it into a "proposals" nested group or mention it in chat.
2. **During a short recurring review**, editors promote accepted channels into their real categories and remove rejected ones. Ten minutes covers 10-15 nominations.
3. **The link updates automatically** — nothing to redistribute, nothing to announce beyond "done#".

For communities (a Discord server with a vetted-creators list, for instance), the pattern is the same with the link pinned in a channel: members open it, the curation team maintain it, and trust grows because "the list" is live and unambiguous.

## What about onboarding new members?

This is one of the quietest productivity wins. A new team member's first-week reading list — "which channels/creators are our sources?" — becomes one link instead of a doc + spreadsheet + tribal knowledge. They open the link, optionally copy the collection into their own workspace, and start exploring immediately.

## Keep your data yours

Export always matters for teams: if your agency reorganizes, or your community restarts its curation, you can get data out. Groupify supports exporting your groups, and because share links are just URLs into your own workspace, you are never locked behind a closed API to publish the data you curated.

**The summary:** make a group, choose the roles, give the link to viewers, and use it as the one non-negotiable source of truth for your channels. Adoption is usually instant once your team sees the difference between a stale spreadsheet and a live list.
